Our Team

Three people who came back home to do this work.

In a region this size, you are not hiring a firm. You are hiring the people who show up. Here is who shows up, and what they did before this.

Evan Jennings
Founder · Quantify Consulting

Evan holds an MBA from Carnegie Mellon in finance, operations, and data, and spent the core of his career as a business intelligence engineer at Amazon Web Services, building the reporting and data infrastructure that a business at that scale runs on. Before and around that: data warehousing, ETL, visualization, and program management for organizations from early-stage startups to global enterprises.

He brought that back to Lewis County on purpose. The problems here are the same ones he solved at AWS, minus the headcount and the budget, and nobody was solving them for a county of 26,000 people. That gap is the whole reason Quantify exists.

Conner Biolsi
Principal Solutions Architect · Quantify Consulting

Conner spent his most recent years as IT Director for Lewis County government, where he rebuilt and modernized the county's entire infrastructure stack, enabled secure remote work across every department, and materially strengthened its cybersecurity posture. He has sat in the chair our clients sit in, which is why he is the one who can tell a county administrator what an assessment is actually going to find.

Allen Beaujon

Why we do this: we hunt waste

Human time and energy are the most precious resources any organization has, meant to be invested, never squandered. People weren't made to be buried in chaos, inefficiency, and systems that grind them down. We were made to create, connect, and contribute, to do work that means something.

So we hunt waste. Not just wasted money or materials, but wasted human potential. Every broken process and unnecessary friction point is stealing something from someone: their time, their energy, their capacity to show up fully for the people and work they love.

We exist to give that back.

Where the waste hides

Most of the organizations we meet are drowning in spreadsheets, forms, and disconnected software, doing the same work three times because nothing is connected. The cost isn't just hours. It's leaders firefighting instead of leading, departments arguing over dueling spreadsheets instead of working from one shared reality, and good people spending their best energy on busywork a system should handle.

A county, an authority, a school district, and a forty-person contractor all have this problem, and none of them have a data team to hand it to. That is the job we do: embed with one team, figure out how the work really happens, redesign it, and automate the repetitive parts.

What we give back

Leverage. More output, more consistency, no extra headcount, and people freed up to do the work only people can do. When a process stops stealing time, that time goes back to residents, to customers, to craft, and to the lives our clients are building outside of work.
